# Trails — what earns a new surface, and how it is read once earned

A trail is a specific pointer from the surface you read to one you haven't.
It earns an *ask*, never an automatic open (SKILL.md, step 5). The same ask
applies when the host cannot read that surface: name the connection needed
and both payoffs; the user decides. A closed surface never cancels a trail —
coverage lists it as unconnected, asked. Each earned surface is read through
two lenses, in this order:

- **Correlate** — bounded probes that support or kill the patterns you already hold. Cheap, and it tells the owner whether the first pass was right.
- **Mine** — the same bounded first pass the first surface got, in this surface's own unit. This is where new patterns come from; the first surface's ceiling is the coordination work it shows.

Then one merged shape check. Name new trail types when you meet them; every
unfollowed trail goes in the coverage section.

## Automation footers and bot posts

Recurring posts signed by a workflow or bot, scheduled digests, "posted by".

- **Points to:** the automation estate — the workflow or job definitions, their run history, the replies under their posts.
- **Correlate:** which of your patterns the automation already covers, and how well — count the corrections and complaints under its output.
- **Mine:** unit = a workflow or scheduled job. Manual fixes after runs, re-posts, hand corrections of its data, jobs nobody reads.
- **Ask:** "3 recurring posts here come from workflow X and 6 replies call its numbers wrong — reading the workflow and its runs tells me whether the fault is the workflow or its data, and what else runs there."

## Artifact gravity

Three or more units orbit one document, board, sheet, or page.

- **Points to:** that artifact and its neighbours.
- **Correlate:** is it fresh, owned, and does it hold what people asked for. A discovery failure (the answer existed, nobody found it) is automatable; an absence (no document exists) is a gap for the report, not a proposal.
- **Mine:** unit = a page, row, or record. What is edited by hand on a cadence, pasted in from elsewhere, or asked about again.
- **Ask:** "Half the doc questions end at that Notion space — checking it tells me whether the docs are missing or just unfound, and what the space itself repeats."

## Relay chain

One object carried by hand across two or more systems: thread into ticket,
doc into sheet, email into CRM.

- **Points to:** the second system.
- **Correlate:** count the hops and who carries them; does the object arrive intact; how long does it wait between systems.
- **Mine:** unit = a ticket or record. What repeats inside that system on its own, independent of the first surface.
- **Ask:** "11 threads were re-typed into the tracker by hand — reading the tracker tells me which fields travel, and what the tracker's own routine looks like."

## Dispute on automated output

"The numbers look wrong", "the bot got it wrong", a human redoing what a
machine posted.

- **Points to:** the automation and its data source.
- **Correlate:** how often the output is corrected by hand; is the failure in the automation or in its input.
- **Mine:** unit = a run or report. Which corrections repeat.
- **Ask:** "6 of 14 leaderboard posts drew a correction — reading the workflow and the source table tells me whether to fix the query or the data, and what else that workflow's runs get corrected by hand."

## Lag between a change and later questions

Something ships; the questions about it arrive days or weeks later.

- **Points to:** the change stream — merged changes, release notes, announcements.
- **Correlate:** match the questions to the changes that caused them.
- **Mine:** unit = a change. What has to be told to whom after each one — the repeated hand-off or announcement.
- **Ask:** "A UX change merges and this channel asks 'bug or by design?' one to two weeks later — reading merged changes tells me which ones will raise questions before they do, and what has to be told to whom after every merge."

## Your own record with this user

The surface points back at you — a ritual they ask you to run, a channel
where your output lands, a report you wrote — or step 1 offered the record
and they took it.

- **Points to:** the host's session store or chat history, the memory you keep for them, your past reports and the verdicts on them.
- **Correlate:** does the pattern you hold on their channel also arrive as an ask to you — the same lookup, relay, or prep requested of the agent by hand? Count those asks and date them against the channel's units.
- **Mine:** unit = a session, read through a condensed view the merger generates (typed asks, tools called, files written); the raw log opens only to verify. What they ask again, what they paste in, what you run for them on a cadence, what the host runs on a schedule and how often it fails. "Resolved by" names what actually ran, not what was called.
- **Ask:** "Fifteen pre-call sheets in 26 days arrived here as typed asks — reading my own sessions tells me what else you hand me on a cadence, and the calendar tells me which calls never got one."
